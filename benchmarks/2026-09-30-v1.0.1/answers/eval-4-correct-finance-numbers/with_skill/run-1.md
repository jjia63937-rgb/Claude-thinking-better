**Yes, both numbers are right.** One claim needs a small caveat, which I've explained below.

**1. The promo price is $40.**
- $40 × 1.25 = $50 after the January increase
- $50 × 0.80 = $40 with the March promo

A 25% increase followed by a 20% discount cancels out exactly, because 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00. It works because both percentages apply in sequence to the new price, which is what you described. Promo customers pay the same $40 a month as last year.

**2. The promo costs $12,000/month compared with the post-increase price.**
- $50 − $40 = $10 discount per customer
- 1,200 × $10 = $12,000/month
- Check: 1,200 × $50 = $60,000 and 1,200 × $40 = $48,000. The difference is $12,000.

**The caveat.** "Revenue per promo customer is unchanged" is true only compared with last year's price. Your second point measures the promo against the $50 price, so a finance lead may ask which baseline you mean. Also, the $12,000 is the gross discount. It's only the real cost if all 1,200 customers would have paid $50 without the promo. Some may have signed up or stayed only because of it.

Suggested wording: *"Net effect: promo customers pay $40/month, the same as last year's price (+25% then −20% cancels exactly). Measured against the new $50 price, the promo is $10/customer, or $12,000/month in discounts across 1,200 promo customers. That assumes all of them would otherwise pay full price."*
