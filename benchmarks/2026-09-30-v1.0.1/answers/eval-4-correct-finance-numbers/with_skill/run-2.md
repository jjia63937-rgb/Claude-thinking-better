**Yes, the math is right, but two phrases need tightening.** Both assume every promo customer pays the $40 promo price monthly, with no other discounts or billing differences.

**The numbers check out:**
- January increase: $40 × 1.25 = **$50**
- Promo: $50 × 0.80 = **$40**
- Checked in reverse: to undo a 25% rise you divide by 1.25, which is the same as multiplying by 0.8. So a 20% discount exactly cancels a 25% increase. This case happens to cancel exactly, even though "+25% then −20%" looks like it shouldn't.
- Promo cost: ($50 − $40) × 1,200 = **$12,000/month** below the post-increase list price.

**Wording I'd tighten before a finance lead reads it:**

1. **"the same $40 they paid last year"** is only literally true for promo customers who were also subscribers last year. Anyone who signed up after January, or who came in through the promo, didn't pay $40 last year. A safer version: "the promo price is $40, the same as last year's list price."

2. **"costs us $12,000/month"** is the gap from list price. It is not necessarily revenue we actually lost. It equals real forgone revenue only if all 1,200 would have stayed and paid $50 without the promo. If some joined or stayed *because* of the promo, the true cost is lower, and could even be negative. A finance lead is likely to push on this. A safer version: "$12,000/month in discounts versus the $50 list price."

"Revenue per promo customer is unchanged" is fine *compared with last year's price*. Just make clear that the comparison is with last year, because compared with the current $50 price it's $10 lower. That's the whole point of your $12,000 figure, so the two sentences can look contradictory unless you name the baseline in each.

**Suggested rewrite:**
> Net effect: the promo price ($50 × 0.8 = $40) equals last year's $40 list price, so for promo customers the discount fully offsets the January increase. Versus the new $50 list price, the promo represents $10/customer/month, or $12,000/month across our 1,200 promo customers, in discounts. Actual forgone revenue depends on how many would have paid $50 without the promo.
