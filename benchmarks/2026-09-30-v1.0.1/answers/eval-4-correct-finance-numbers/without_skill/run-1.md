Yes, your numbers are right.

**The promo price:**
- January increase: $40 × 1.25 = **$50/month**
- March promo: $50 × 0.80 = **$40/month**

Promo customers pay exactly $40, the same as last year. The two changes cancel here because 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00. (A 25% increase followed by a 25% discount would not cancel. It would give $37.50.)

**The promo cost:**
- Discount per customer: $50 − $40 = $10/month
- 1,200 × $10 = **$12,000/month** compared with the post-increase price

**Two wording points your finance lead might raise:**
1. **"Revenue per promo customer is unchanged"** is true for list price compared with last year. It holds only if nothing else changed, such as the plan tier, billing terms or payment fees.
2. **"The promo costs us $12,000/month"** is revenue given up compared with charging $50, not cash spent. It assumes all 1,200 customers would have paid $50 without the promo. Some might have churned or never signed up, so the real cost is probably lower. Calling it "$12,000/month in discounts vs. the $50 list price" would be more precise.

You can send it as written. The two changes above just make it harder to pick apart.
