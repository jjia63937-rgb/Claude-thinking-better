**You can't. No speed on the last 60 km is fast enough to bring your average for the whole trip up to 60 km/h.**

Here's why:

- To average 60 km/h over 120 km, the whole trip has to take **120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours**.
- The first 60 km at 30 km/h already took **60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours**.

So your full time budget is gone, and you would need to cover the remaining 60 km in zero time.

The trap is that the answer looks like 90 km/h, because (30 + 90) / 2 = 60. That doesn't work, because average speed is total distance divided by total time, not the mean of the two speeds. At 90 km/h the second half takes 40 minutes, so the trip takes 2 h 40 min and your average is 120 ÷ 2.67 ≈ **45 km/h**.

What's still possible:

| Speed on the last 60 km | Total time | Overall average |
|---|---|---|
| 60 km/h | 3 h | 40 km/h |
| 90 km/h | 2 h 40 min | 45 km/h |
| 120 km/h | 2 h 30 min | 48 km/h |

Even at an unrealistic speed you would stay under 60 km/h, and you would only get close to it. Driving well above the speed limit to make up lost time gains you much less than it seems to.
