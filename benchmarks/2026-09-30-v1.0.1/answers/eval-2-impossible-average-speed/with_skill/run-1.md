**You can't. No speed on the remaining 60 km will bring the whole trip up to a 60 km/h average.**

Here's why:

- Averaging 60 km/h over 120 km means the whole trip has to take **120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours**.
- The first 60 km at 30 km/h already took **60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours**.

You've already used all 2 hours, so you'd have to cover the last 60 km in zero time. The average gets closer to 60 km/h the faster you go, but it never reaches it:

| Speed for the last 60 km | Total time | Average for the trip |
|---|---|---|
| 60 km/h | 3 h | 40 km/h |
| 90 km/h | 2 h 40 min | 45 km/h |
| 120 km/h | 2.5 h | 48 km/h |

The quick answer "90 km/h", which averages 30 and 90, doesn't work. Average speed is total distance divided by total time. You spent much longer at the slow speed, so it counts for more.
