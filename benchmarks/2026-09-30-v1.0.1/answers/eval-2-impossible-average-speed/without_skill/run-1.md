Unfortunately, no speed will get you there. It isn't possible.

To average 60 km/h over 120 km, the whole trip has to take **120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours**.

You drove the first 60 km at 30 km/h, which took **60 ÷ 30 = 2 hours**. That's the entire time budget gone, and you still have 60 km to drive.

Any time spent on the last 60 km, however short, pushes the total over 2 hours. That means your average will always come in under 60 km/h. You'd have to cover the remaining distance in zero time, which would take infinite speed.

Some examples of what's realistic for the second half:

| Speed on last 60 km | Time for last 60 km | Total time | Overall average |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 km/h | 1 h | 3 h | 40 km/h |
| 90 km/h | 40 min | 2 h 40 min | 45 km/h |
| 120 km/h | 30 min | 2.5 h | 48 km/h |

The trap is that averaging 30 and 90 to get 60 doesn't work. Average speed is total distance divided by total time, and you spend far more time at the slow speed than at the fast one.
