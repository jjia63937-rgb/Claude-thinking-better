Unfortunately, it can't be done. No speed is fast enough.

**Why:** Averaging 60 km/h over 120 km means finishing the whole trip in **2 hours** (120 ÷ 60).

You drove the first 60 km at 30 km/h, which took **2 hours** (60 ÷ 30). That's your entire time budget. You'd have to cover the last 60 km in zero time, which would take infinite speed.

**Why the intuition fails:** It's tempting to say "30 and 90 average to 60, so drive 90 km/h." But average speed is total distance divided by total time, not the average of the speeds. The slow half takes longer, so it counts for more. At 90 km/h the second half takes 40 minutes, for a total of 2 h 40 min and an average of about 45 km/h.

**What you can still get:**

| Speed for last 60 km | Total time | Overall average |
|---|---|---|
| 60 km/h | 3 h | 40 km/h |
| 90 km/h | 2 h 40 min | 45 km/h |
| 120 km/h | 2 h 30 min | 48 km/h |

Even with infinite speed, the most you could ever average is 60 km/h, and you'd only reach it in the limit. In practice, drive at a safe, legal speed. Going faster wins back surprisingly little time.
