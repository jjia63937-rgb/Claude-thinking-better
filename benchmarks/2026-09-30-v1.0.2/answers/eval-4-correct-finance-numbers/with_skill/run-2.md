**Yes, your numbers are right, assuming promo customers paid the full $40 list price last year and the promo is billed monthly with no other discounts.**

- **Promo price:** $40 × 1.25 = $50. Then $50 × 0.80 = $40. So promo customers pay the same $40 as last year. It works out exactly because 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00. (A 25% discount would have overshot to $37.50. It takes a 20% discount to exactly undo a 25% increase.)
- **Promo cost:** $50 − $40 = $10 per customer per month. $10 × 1,200 = **$12,000/month** below the post-increase price. As a check, 1,200 × $50 = $60,000 and 1,200 × $40 = $48,000, which is also a $12,000 difference.

Two wording points a finance lead may raise. The math doesn't change, but the claims could be read more broadly than you mean them:

1. **"Costs us $12,000/month" is foregone revenue measured against list price.** It assumes all 1,200 customers would have paid $50 without the promo. If some of them signed up or stayed only because of the promo, the real cost is lower. Consider writing "$12,000/month below list price" or "$12,000/month in discounts."
2. **"Revenue per promo customer is unchanged" means compared with last year.** It's true for March onward. In January and February those customers paid $50. It's also only unchanged compared with last year: against the new $50 price, each promo customer brings in $10 less.

With those two clarifications, the email is accurate.
